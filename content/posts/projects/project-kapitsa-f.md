This is a project for MIT 2.671 (Instrument and Measurement) - a class about observing and measuring physical variables, such as force, strain, temperature, flow rate, etc. As a part of this class, we get to pick our own topic of interests for measurement, as long as you have a way to measure it. 

The idea for my project comes by after seeing a [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gMAKamGIiMc) of a column of fluid being suspended by vibration. It turns out this system belongs a class of unstable system where stability can be achieved through vibration. It is quite fascinating to me that you can stabilize some systems with such an unorthodox method, and that feedback control is not always the only solution to control an unstable dynamical system. 

The system that I pick to demonstrate this effect (due to ease of setting up and demo) is called the "Kapitza's pendulum": an inverted pendulum system where you can stabilize by vibrating its pivot point vertically.  

Here's a video demo of the phenomenon, and my written works on the project that goes into more details.
